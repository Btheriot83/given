Named

Privacy Policy

Effective October 24, 2026. Applies to Named for iPhone and the Named web game.

Named is a daily name-guessing game. It is built to need as little of your data as possible.

No account

Named has no sign-up and no login. We never ask for your name, email address, contacts, location or photos.

Your games and stats stay on your device

Your guesses, streaks, statistics and settings are saved only on your iPhone (or in your browser’s storage on the web). On iPhone, your Name Journal and streak freezes are kept the same way. Apart from the anonymous puzzle results described under “Comparing your results”, they are not uploaded. Deleting the app, or clearing the site’s data, removes them.

Your Names (iPhone)

You can save your own name and the names of people you love so the app can mark a daily puzzle that matches one of them. These names are stored only on your iPhone. They are never sent to namedgame.com or anyone else, and they are never included in share text. Remove them any time in Settings → Names that matter to you; deleting the app removes them too.

Purchases (iPhone)

Named+ is sold through Apple’s App Store. Apple processes the payment; we never see your card or Apple ID details. We use RevenueCat to check whether Named+ is unlocked. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app user ID created on your device together with your App Store purchase receipts, so it can confirm and restore your purchase. It is not linked to your identity and is not used for advertising.

The friends board (optional)

If you join a friends board, the game sends that board’s invite code, the nickname you choose, a random player ID created on your device, and your guesses for the official daily puzzle. The server checks the guesses to work out your score and stores only your nickname and score for that board and day. Entries are deleted automatically after 90 days. Invite codes are unlisted, not private accounts: anyone with a code can see that board’s nicknames and scores, so don’t use your full name.

Comparing your results

After you finish a numbered daily puzzle, the game sends namedgame.com a random install ID created on your device, the puzzle’s date and your guesses. The server checks the guesses and stores only your score for that number (1–6, or a miss), plus a running total of how many numbered puzzles you’ve played, how many you solved and your average. That is how we can show how everyone did on a puzzle and how you rank overall. It is anonymous: there is no name, email or account, we do not store IP addresses, and the install ID is not linked to your identity. Each score is kept with the install ID so a puzzle counts only once per device. Your running totals are deleted automatically after 400 days without play.

Measuring our App Store ads (iPhone)

We advertise Named with Apple Search Ads. To learn whether those ads work, the iPhone app sends namedgame.com two things the first time it opens: Apple’s anonymous ad-attribution token (from Apple’s AdServices framework) and a random install ID created on your device. Our server asks Apple what that token means; Apple replies only whether the install came from a Search Ads campaign and, if so, which campaign, ad group, keyword, country and click date. If you buy Named+, the app also sends a “purchased” flag with the same install ID. We use this only to count installs and purchases per campaign. It includes no name, email or device identifiers such as the advertising identifier; we do not store IP addresses, do not combine it with other data and do not track you across apps or websites. Records are deleted automatically after 400 days.

Reminders (iPhone)

If you turn on the daily reminder, it is scheduled on your iPhone by iOS. Nothing about it is sent to us.

No tracking, no ads in the game

Named shows no ads, does not track you across apps or websites, does not use analytics SDKs and does not sell or share data with data brokers. The only measurement is the anonymous ad-attribution count described above.

Name facts

The name facts come from the U.S. Social Security Administration’s public national baby-name files, bundled with the game. Looking them up happens on your device.

Children

Named is suitable for all ages and does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and its date. Questions, or deletion requests for a friends-board entry or your puzzle results: support@namedgame.com.
